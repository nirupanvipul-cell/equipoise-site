Ventilation strategy in ARDS and the ventilated ICU patient — what the trials actually compared

◆ marks a sentence that is this document's own synthesis from the map, or a next-study judgement — verified for consistency with the map, not for external support (SHEET-7, P-83). Everything unmarked is fact and is verified strictly.

The question as usually asked: how should I ventilate this ARDS patient? ◆ This document refuses the question's shape twice. First, 'lung-protective ventilation' is a bundle, and the verified spine below shows its components answering differently — low tidal volume alone: five single-component rows in the spine below (a sixth, Karalapillai, is an operating-room trial and lives in that document), with ARMA's mortality benefit and Determann's lung-injury benefit the only two positives and null or a harm-leaning null in the rest, added fixed intensity never positive in three trials, and every guided titration strategy yet tried null or, in ART, harmful. Second, the recent literature is not asking that question any more: the open-layer tier shows it asking HOW to titrate — by imaging, by oesophageal pressure, by driving pressure — in trials too small to carry an outcome claim. The two tiers are rendered separately because one is owner-verified and one is machine-classified, and this document never lets the second borrow the first's authority.

The walkthrough — what the two tiers say together

◆ The verified spine answers the composed question. Against a comparator with none of the bundle, the bundle won once and decisively (Amato 1998: 38% versus 71% mortality, in 53 analysed). One component alone — low tidal volume against traditional volumes — carries a mortality benefit in ARDS once (ARMA, 31.0% versus 39.8%) and, outside ARDS, one benefit on lung-injury development rather than mortality (Determann, 150 patients); Stewart and Brower are null and Brochard is a harm-leaning null on the same contrast, and low-versus-intermediate volume is null in 961 (PReVENT). The PEEP floor is non-inferior at lower levels outside ARDS (RELAx). Adding fixed higher PEEP to low tidal volume is null in ALVEOLI and EXPRESS and a benefit-leaning null in LOVS (the map's label: null-trend-benefit). Every guided titration strategy yet tried — staircase recruitment with decremental PEEP (ART), sustained maximal recruitment (PHARLAP), oesophageal-pressure guidance against an empirical high-PEEP table (EPVent-2), morphology-personalised strategy (LIVE) — is null except ART, whose verified direction is harm (28-day mortality 55.3% versus 49.3%, HR 1.20, CI 1.01–1.42). ◆ The heterogeneity the owner's mechanism predicts is real at the alveolar scale; what the verified map shows is that no titration signal yet used to exploit it has converted that mechanism into survival.

◆ The open tier shows where the field has gone. Most of the rendered open-tier trials are titration-METHOD or mode comparisons — electrical impedance tomography against tables, transpulmonary pressure against empirical settings, APRV against conventional modes, sigh breaths, spontaneous-effort strategies — run at 16 to 258 patients against physiological endpoints. ◆ Read against the verified spine, the pattern is uncomfortable: the field is refining HOW to individualise while no individualised strategy in either tier of this map has improved a patient-important outcome — the only strategy that moved a patient-important outcome — ART — moved 28-day mortality toward harm (55.3% versus 49.3%, HR 1.20, CI 1.01–1.42), and the open tier's positive readings are physiological endpoints, and a 700-randomised contrast of pressure control with preserved spontaneous ventilation reads flat on its extracted numbers (120/347 against 118/352). The map cannot say whether these methods work; it can say, cell by cell, that nobody has yet run the trial that would.

The axes themselves are a finding (P-113)

◆ Derived from four independent sources rather than authored: across 15 trial-named, 7 review-named and 13 guideline-named candidate axes, only prone positioning clears the three-source bar. Tidal volume, PEEP, driving-pressure limitation, neuromuscular blockade and ECMO are each named by trials and guidelines, and sedation by trials and reviews, without any axis but prone crossing three sources — the synthesis literature is not cutting this evidence where the trials and the guidelines cut it. Ventilatory mode is named by the trials source alone. The disagreement is rendered rather than resolved.

The next study this map implies (computed from the map)

◆ The trial this map is missing is not another small titration-method comparison — the open tier is full of them. It is an outcome-powered trial of ANY individualised pressure strategy against best fixed practice, in the stratum the mechanism predicts should separate (moderate-to-severe ARDS, paralysed, prone-eligible), with achieved separation measured and reported per arm — because F3, the adherence prediction, is untestable today for the plain reason that no trial extraction carries achieved-versus-intended titration. Until that trial exists, the verified spine's answer stands: protect, keep the tidal volume low, and spend no confidence on any particular way of being clever about pressure.
