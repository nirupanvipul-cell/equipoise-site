Ventilation strategy in the operating room — what the trials actually compared (r4)

◆ marks a sentence that is this document's own synthesis from the map, or a next-study judgement — verified for consistency with the map, not for external support (SHEET-7, P-83). Everything unmarked is fact and is verified strictly.

The question as usually asked: what ventilator settings for surgery? This document splits the question by what was actually compared, in fourteen randomised trials in the operating room. ◆ The pattern is not one answer: protection against nothing is decisively good, adding fixed intensity to existing protection is consistently null, and individualizing the pressure side is the one live question, with its single strong positive sitting in one-lung surgery.

Where the evidence sits (not what to do)

Lever Where the evidence sits
Tidal volume ◆ No later trial in this map repeated IMPROVE's zero-PEEP, no-recruitment comparator; the one later comparator arm at conventional tidal volumes (Karalapillai's) sat at PEEP 5 in both arms. Low tidal volume alone, with PEEP held equal at 5, was null in 1,206 analysed mixed major-surgery patients (Karalapillai 2020) — the map's one single-component tidal-volume contrast.
Driving pressure ◆ As a TITRATION TARGET it has one positive trial (thoracic 2019, n=292), one large null (thoracic multicentre 2023, n=1,170), and nulls in cardiac, laparoscopic, elective-abdominal and emergency-abdominal surgery — the emergency trial trending the wrong way (IMPROVE-2, RR 1.27, CI 0.96–1.68). No trial has tested driving pressure as a LIMIT rather than a titration target in the OR.
PEEP ◆ Fixed higher PEEP with routine recruitment, added to a protective base, is null in every population tried — open abdomen (PROVHILO), obesity (PROBESE), one-lung (PROTHOR) — at a haemodynamic price where measured. Individualized PEEP is the live question: one strongly positive one-lung trial (iPROVE-OLV 2024), nulls elsewhere.
Recruitment manoeuvres ◆ Never tested alone in this map: in every trial that added fixed higher PEEP (PROVHILO, PROBESE, PROTHOR) recruitment manoeuvres came packaged with it, so those trials cannot separate the two. The one trial with no recruitment in either arm (Karalapillai 2020) was a tidal-volume contrast.

◆ Setting is the first split, before any component. These are paralysed, supine or positioned lungs, ventilated for hours not days, with atelectasis rather than inflammation as the dominant mechanism — and the primary outcomes in this map are postoperative complication measures — IMPROVE-2 folds death into its composite, none is standalone mortality — which is why this document is separate from the ICU-ARDS one and their answers must not be transplanted.

◆ Read in the map's own order. Protection against no protection settles first: IMPROVE 2013, whose comparator arm was ventilated at 11.1±1.1 mL/kg (as extracted) with zero PEEP and no recruitment, cut its composite complication rate from 27.5% to 10.5% — and no later trial in this map repeated that comparator; the one later above-8 comparator (Karalapillai's conventional arm) kept PEEP 5 in both arms. One component alone teaches next: the map's single tidal-volume-only contrast (Karalapillai 2020) is null in 1,206 analysed. Adding fixed intensity, on this map, is a three-trial null: PROVHILO, PROBESE and PROTHOR could not make fixed higher PEEP with routine recruitment pay on top of a protective base. Individualization is where the argument now lives: five populations carry a trial and one (obesity) carries none; one strong positive (one-lung, iPROVE-OLV), one small early positive (DP-thoracic 2019), and null or worse in the rest, with the emergency abdomen's point estimate on the harm side. The one-lung PEEP cells put 'individualized' only on the positive side (iPROVE-OLV), while 'DP-titrated' sits on both — a small positive (DP-thoracic 2019) and the large null (DP-thoracic multicentre 2023) — so whether the individualized label rather than DP-titration carries the effect is the sharpest open question the map contains.

What this room teaches the other one

◆ The verified ICU rows of the shared ventilation map show the same structure from a different lung: adding fixed higher PEEP to low tidal volume is null there three times (ALVEOLI, LOVS, EXPRESS), and every guided titration strategy yet tried there is null or harmful (ART harmed). What does NOT transfer: the OR outcomes in this map are postoperative pulmonary complications in atelectatic lungs; the verified ICU rows' are mortality and ventilator-free-day outcomes in inflamed ones. A titration method that pays in one-lung surgery has not thereby earned a place in ARDS — the ARDS appraisal is its own document (EQ-ICU-ARDS).

Is another trial worth running? (the next-study computation)

◆ The next OR trial worth running is not another fixed-PEEP arm — three large nulls already answer it. It is a head-to-head of the two titration labels this map's own one-lung cells carry — 'individualized' against 'DP-titrated' — powered for severe pulmonary complications at the 6% versus 15% separation iPROVE-OLV observed, with haemodynamic cost as a co-primary rather than an afterthought, because where this map's stored cost numbers stand unchallenged (DESIGNATION), hypotension (382/718 vs 317/717) and vasopressor use (224/718 vs 130/717) run against the DP-guided high-PEEP arm; the laparoscopic trial's stored numbers are disputed by the external checker and withheld, so cost belongs beside the primary, assumed in neither direction. Obesity is the untested population in this map: none of its rows tests individualized PEEP there.
