Ventilation strategy — what the trials actually compared

◆ marks a sentence that is this document's own synthesis from the map, or a next-study judgement — verified for consistency with the map, not for external support (SHEET-7, P-83). Everything unmarked is fact and is verified strictly.

The question as usually asked: is lung-protective ventilation valuable? This document answers it by refusing to treat it as one question. 'Protective ventilation' is a bundle — low tidal volume, PEEP, recruitment manoeuvres — and the thirty randomised trials below compared different pieces of it against different comparators in different lungs. ◆ Read cell by cell, the literature is not a diluted null; it is several sharp answers to several different questions, and the walkthrough below arrives at the question behind the one asked.

◆ Reading the map. Against no protection at all, the bundle — or most of it — wins: in ARDS, Amato's full bundle (low tidal volume with high PEEP and recruitment, confirmed in the full text), and in at-risk abdominal surgery the full bundle (IMPROVE, major pulmonary and extrapulmonary complications 10.5% versus 27.5%). But no modern trial in this map still uses those comparators — 12 mL/kg with minimal PEEP, or zero PEEP — so those wins date themselves. One component alone, tested cleanly at scale, moves nothing outside ARDS (Determann's 150-patient early-stopped trial, where low tidal volume alone reduced new lung injury, is the one smaller exception): low tidal volume with PEEP held equal was null in 1,206 surgical patients (Karalapillai) and in 961 ICU patients without ARDS (PReVENT) — with the one great exception that in established ARDS, low tidal volume alone reduced mortality in 861 patients (ARMA), the one setting where a single component carries a decisive result. The heterogeneity the common question complains about is not noise; it is the comparator and the population changing from trial to trial.

◆ Then the pattern that settles most of the argument: adding fixed high PEEP with routine recruitment to lungs already protectively ventilated did not reduce the complications it aimed at, and cost circulation every time it was tried (though it did lift some secondary respiratory measures) — open abdomen (PROVHILO), obesity (PROBESE), one-lung ventilation (PROTHOR) — and the driving-pressure-guided high-PEEP variant fared no better in open abdomen (DESIGNATION). Pushed to its aggressive extreme in ARDS, the same idea probably does harm: in ART, staircase recruitment's arm died more often at 28 days (55.3% versus 49.3%, HR 1.20, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.42). And in emergency abdominal surgery, maximal individualized PEEP's primary outcome was null (IMPROVE-2, 25.7% versus 20.2%); within its secondaries the reintubation-or-rescue component ran against the intervention — a caution attached to a null, not a conclusion.

◆ What survives is narrower and more interesting: individualized titration in the most collapsible lungs. In one-lung ventilation, PEEP titrated to the lowest driving pressure (5.5% versus 12.2% complications) and an individualized open-lung protocol (6% versus 15%) both won — while the same idea has so far been null in cardiac, laparoscopic and non-selected thoracic surgery. The question behind the question is not "is protective ventilation valuable"; it is "which lungs need their PEEP found rather than set" — and the honest answer today is: the isolated ventilated lung, probably; other settings, unproven.

The pre-registered hypothesis, tested

Before this map was built, the owner's mechanism and predictions were written down and timestamped in this corpus's own register so the map could test them rather than absorb them — an internal pre-registration, part of the stored evidence profile, not an external citation. Per prediction:

  • benefit confined to full-bundle cells — REFUTED as universal; PARTIALLY CONFIRMED intraoperatively. ARMA (PEEP by the same tables in both arms) and Determann (PEEP not protocolised, and identified as an independent predictor in its own analysis) show low-tidal-volume-alone benefit in ICU strata; in the intraoperative stratum no single-component contrast shows benefit (Karalapillai null) and every intraoperative benefit cell is bundle-vs-nothing (IMPROVE) or an individualized-PEEP bundle (iPROVE-OLV, DP-guided thoracic 2019); Amato's bundle-vs-nothing benefit is an ICU cell.
  • partial-bundle cells do worse than high tidal volume — REFUTED. no partial-bundle cell shows worse-than-high-TV; the harm signals sit in AGGRESSIVE addition on top of a protective base (ART mortality HR 1.20; IMPROVE-2 reintubation RR 1.97; PHARLAP arrhythmia), not in omission.
  • larger effects in high-risk abdominal, obesity, Trendelenburg, laparoscopy — PARTIALLY REFUTED / UNDECIDABLE. the direct obesity test (PROBESE, PEEP+RM addition) is null; high-risk abdominal benefit appears only against a nothing-comparator (IMPROVE) and not against a protective base (PROVHILO, DESIGNATION, PRIME-AIR null); laparoscopy test underpowered; no Trendelenburg-specific cell exists.
  • pragmatic single-component trials cluster at null — CONFIRMED. every large single-component contrast is null: Karalapillai, PReVENT, Stewart, Brower, Brochard trend, RELAx noninferior.

The mechanism's components were found in the literature: low-tidal-volume ventilation causes derecruitment that a recruitment manoeuvre reverses (Richard 2001, PMID: 11401882, verified), and per-alveolus pressure-volume heterogeneity is a real measurement literature. The composite quantitative claim — the spread widening over time, recruitment shifting every unit's opening pressure left — appears nowhere as stated and is recorded as the owner's own inference. ◆ The map's verdict on the mechanism: it survives in its individualized form only — routine fixed recruitment, which the mechanism as stated would endorse, is null in the three large fixed-strategy trials and the guided variant, and did harm in its aggressive forms.

Is another trial worth running? (the next-study computation)

◆ Is another randomisation worth its patients? The judgements below are qualitative feasibility judgements from the map's own event rates and trial sizes — no formal computation is claimed; the decision-theoretic frame they approximate is expected value of sample information — EVSI (Ades, Lu and Claxton, Medical Decision Making 2004, PMID: 15090106) — and the full computation is registered future work (R5). Per open cell:

  • ◆ Low tidal volume in ARDS — settled by ARMA against a comparator that no longer exists; no feasible trial of that contrast could change management.
  • ◆ Fixed high PEEP plus routine recruitme on a protective base** — four large nulls with consistent haemodynamic cost make the accrued information decisive against effect sizes of the magnitude these trials were designed to find; another fixed-strategy trial would not change management and is not worth the patients.
  • ◆ Aggressive recruitment in ARDS — a mortality harm signal is its own stopping rule.
  • ◆ Mortality for any intraoperative strategy — every intraoperative trial in this map took a complication outcome, not mortality, as its primary (the map's primary-outcome column shows it, with event rates from about 6% to 56%), and none reported a mortality benefit, so a mortality-powered trial would dwarf this set's largest (2,200 randomised); as with the cricoid-pressure trial, a study that large would be clinically useless even if statistically positive — the shape of the cricoid-pressure trial (IRIS, 3,472 patients, PMID: 30347104), the owner's worked example for this judgement. The decision rests on the complication signal and the mechanism.
  • ◆ Individualized PEEP in elective one-lung ventilation — the one cell where a feasible trial (a few hundred patients at the observed event rates) could genuinely change management: two positive trials, a null multicentre cousin, and a plausible mechanism. This is where the field's next randomisation belongs.

The methods layer

Everything above is argued from the map; everything below is the same evidence in its accountable form.

Ventilation strategy

The evidence divides on what was actually compared, not on what 'lung-protective ventilation' means. Trials that compared the full bundle against no protection found benefit; trials that added fixed high PEEP and routine recruitment to an already protective base found nothing, at a haemodynamic price; and the setting where individualized titration has most clearly shown benefit is one-lung ventilation.

Intraoperative and intensive-care ventilation strategy in adults: tidal volume, driving pressure, PEEP and recruitment, against conventional practice, for death and for pulmonary complications.
The contrast The full bundle against no protection wins; the same components added one at a time to a protective base won none of their primary outcomes — bar one small early trial, Determann.
The number Three large trials of fixed high PEEP with routine recruitment on a protective base — PROVHILO, PROBESE, PROTHOR — and the guided variant DESIGNATION: not one positive primary outcome, and haemodynamic cost in each.
The mechanism Per-alveolus pressure-volume heterogeneity: one setting cannot fit every unit, which is why individualized titration is the surviving form of the idea (the owner's pre-registered mechanism, components confirmed in the literature).
The surprise Adding more protection to protected lungs is not neutral: aggressive recruitment in ARDS increased mortality, and in emergency surgery maximal titration raised the reintubation-or-rescue component of its secondary outcomes.

What the evidence supports

In established ARDS, low tidal volume with a plateau-pressure limit probably reduces mortality against the old 12 mL/kg practice: ARMA, 861 patients, 31.0% versus 39.8% (P=0.007), with PEEP set by the same tables in both arms.

Confidence: probable — for the question: In adults with ARDS, does low tidal volume alone reduce mortality? — one large decisive trial with three small earlier trials null on the same contrast; the comparator tidal volume is no longer used, so the estimate dates itself

  • Observed: ARMA's mortality benefit on a low-tidal-volume-only contrast, with Stewart, Brochard and Brower null on smaller versions of the same contrast.
  • Confounded with: the size of the tidal-volume separation and the era of the comparator arm: 12 mL/kg is not contemporary practice.
  • What would resolve it: not worth another trial: the comparator no longer exists in practice, so no feasible trial of this contrast could change management (the next-study computation is in the clinician layer).

In intermediate-to-high-risk abdominal surgery, the full bundle — low tidal volume with PEEP and recruitment — probably reduces a composite of major pulmonary and extrapulmonary complications against ventilation with none of the three: IMPROVE, 400 patients, 10.5% versus 27.5% (RR 0.40, 95% CI 0.24 to 0.68).

Confidence: probable — for the question: In at-risk abdominal surgery, does the full protective bundle beat wholly unprotective ventilation? — a single multicentre trial; the contrast spans all three components at once, so no single component can be credited

  • Observed: IMPROVE's composite reduction on a bundle-versus-nothing contrast.
  • Confounded with: all three components moved together, with the comparator receiving zero PEEP and 10-12 mL/kg — a comparator now rare in practice.
  • What would resolve it: the informative next trial is not bundle-versus-nothing again; it is which component carries the effect — and the single-component cells below already answer most of that.

Adding fixed high PEEP with routine recruitment to an already protective base probably does not reduce pulmonary complications, and it costs circulation: null in PROVHILO (40% vs 39%), PROBESE (21.3% vs 23.6%) and PROTHOR (53.6% vs 56.4%), with hypotension or vasoactive use higher in each — and the driving-pressure-guided high-PEEP variant was null too (DESIGNATION, 19.8% vs 17.4%), with the same haemodynamic cost.

Confidence: probable — for the question: Does adding fixed high PEEP and routine recruitment to protective ventilation help? — three large fixed-strategy trials plus the guided variant, each null on its primary with the same direction of haemodynamic cost; intervals in each still span some benefit, so probable rather than established

  • Observed: the same null-with-haemodynamic-cost pattern in four independent large trials of the higher-PEEP-plus-recruitment family, three fixed and one guided.
  • Confounded with: nothing material: the replication is across populations and countries; oxygenation consistently favours high PEEP while patient-centred outcomes do not.
  • What would resolve it: no further fixed-high-PEEP trial is worth running: the existing information is decisive at this effect size (next-study computation in the clinician layer).

Aggressive staircase recruitment with compliance-titrated PEEP in moderate-to-severe ARDS probably increases mortality: ART, 1,010 patients, 28-day mortality 55.3% versus 49.3% (HR 1.20, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.42), with more barotrauma.

Confidence: probable — for the question: Is aggressive recruitment safe in ARDS? — one large trial with a harm signal crossing significance, supported directionally by PHARLAP's arrhythmia excess after its early stop

  • Observed: ART's mortality and barotrauma excess; PHARLAP stopped early with more cardiovascular events.
  • Confounded with: the recruitment protocol's aggressiveness — staircase to high pressures — which gentler protocols do not share.
  • What would resolve it: not worth re-testing as designed: a harm signal at this size settles the aggressive form; the open question is gentler, individualized titration, which is the refined question.

Individualized titration has its clearest signal in one-lung ventilation and is not settled even there: driving-pressure-guided PEEP reduced complications in one trial (5.5% versus 12.2%, OR 0.42, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.99) and an individualized open-lung bundle in another (6% versus 15%, RR 0.39, 95% CI 0.28 to 0.56) — while the largest one-lung titration trial was null (1,170 analysed), and the idea is null so far in cardiac, laparoscopic and emergency abdominal surgery.

Confidence: probable — for the question: Where does individualized PEEP titration actually help? — two positive one-lung trials against the largest one-lung trial null and four nulls in other settings; the setting split is suggestive, not settled

  • Observed: two positive one-lung trials and one large null one, with null results elsewhere, on the same titration idea; the positive open-lung result is a bundle, not PEEP titration alone.
  • Confounded with: the one-lung setting bundles titration with an already-protective base and a uniquely collapsible lung; IMPROVE-2's emergency-surgery harm signal (reintubation RR 1.97, 95% CI 1.24 to 3.11) warns against transporting the idea to sicker populations unexamined.
  • What would resolve it: worth one more trial, and only here: individualized versus fixed-low PEEP in elective one-lung ventilation, powered on pulmonary complications — feasible at a few hundred patients given the observed event rates.

A single component alone moved nothing measurable in the large trials that isolated it outside ARDS — with one smaller exception, Determann, where low tidal volume alone reduced new lung injury in 150 patients before an early stop. Low tidal volume with equal PEEP was null in major surgery (Karalapillai, 38% versus 39%, RR 0.97, 95% CI 0.84 to 1.11) and in ICU patients without ARDS (PReVENT, ventilator-free days 21 versus 21), and lower against higher PEEP without ARDS was noninferior (RELAx — a PEEP question, not a tidal-volume one); the small early ICU trials on the tidal-volume contrast (Stewart, Brochard, Brower) were null too.

Confidence: probable — for the question: Is low tidal volume alone worth anything outside ARDS? — three large trials each isolating one component cleanly, each null, with the small early trials in the same direction; the owner's dilution prediction named this pattern before the map was built

  • Observed: clean single-component contrasts at scale, all null.
  • Confounded with: event rates and populations differ across the three; none tested the high-risk strata alone.
  • What would resolve it: no single-component trial is worth running: the question the field should fund is the individualized-titration one above, not another isolated-component null.

Where this leaves the decision
